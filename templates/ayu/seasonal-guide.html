Nature Changes, So Should We

In Ayurveda, the macrocosm (universe) is mirrored in the microcosm (human body). Just as plants bloom in spring and shed in autumn, our internal Doshas accumulate and dissipate with the seasons.

Ritucharya is the practice of adjusting your diet and lifestyle to maintain balance despite external shifts. Transitioning between seasons (Ritu Sandhi) is considered the most critical time for health.

Winter

Vata / Kapha

Agni is highest in winter to combat the cold. Focus on grounding and warmth.

  • Favor soups and hot grains
  • Internal & external oils
  • Avoid cold/raw foods

Spring

Kapha Detox

The "melting" of winter Kapha. Focus on detoxification and renewal.

  • Favor bitter & pungent tastes
  • Light, stimulating exercise
  • Minimize heavy/oily dairy

Summer

Pitta Cooling

External heat raises internal Pitta. Focus on cooling and hydration.

  • Juicy fruits & leafy greens
  • Stay hydrated with coconut water
  • Avoid spicy & fried foods

Autumn

Vata Grounding

Dry and windy weather aggravates Vata. Focus on moisture and stability.

  • Nourishing root vegetables
  • Regular sleep schedule
  • Stay warm and wrap up

Seasonal Dosha Transitions

Each season has a specific relationship with the Doshas, broadly categorized into three phases:

1. Chaya (Accumulation)

The Dosha begins to increase in its own habitat due to environmental factors.

2. Prakopa (Vitiation)

The Dosha overflows its habitat and can lead to immediate symptoms.

3. Prashama (Pacification)

The Dosha naturally returns to its equilibrium as the season passes.
